About

Polyhedra in Polyhedra is an open record book for one family of questions: how small can a polyhedron be and still hold n copies of a polyhedron? It holds the best packings found so far, each with a proof that it is valid, and it is open to better ones from anyone.

How it started

In October 2026 Yohei Nakajima packed 12 unit cubes in a cube of side 2.9315 with a soft-to-rigid method: start every cube as a ball and sharpen it into a cube under pressure. The project began by generalizing that method from cubes to all five Platonic solids, as pieces and as containers (9–11 October 2026, nine problems), with every packing certified in exact arithmetic. On 12 October it grew to every Archimedean and Catalan solid packed in itself and to regular prisms and antiprisms packed in themselves and in a cube, which needed a new soft shape (the r-rounding) for solids without an insphere and a certifier that works in any number field. The search ran in the cloud, on a laptop and on GitHub Actions until every one of the 8,904 cases had a certified packing.

The full account of the Platonic phase, including every idea that did not work, is in the final report.

People and credit

  • Alejandro Zarzuelo Urdiales (@alejandrozu) developed the generalized packing method, building on Yohei Nakajima's soft-to-rigid cube-packing method. He runs the project, updates and maintains the site, and is credited for the packings found by the search.
  • Yohei Nakajima created the soft-to-rigid method and the original simulator, tightening and checker (soft-to-rigid-packing, MIT).
  • Software development used Claude (Anthropic) as a tool under Alejandro Zarzuelo Urdiales's direction.
  • Previously published values come from Erich Friedman's Packing Center, with packings by E. Friedman, R. Walsh, H. Lin and Y. Nakajima, and the all-25-pair coordinate supplement of Berthold, Kamp, Mexi, Pokutta and Pólik's 2026 optimization paper. Published coordinates from Tencent Hunyuan and other attributed sources are retained alongside community constructions. Original authorship and certificate refinements are credited separately in each record.
  • Ryan Xu (@small_LM) published the imported constructions for 34, 35 and 36 cubes in a cube in square_packing.
  • T. Berthold, D. Kamp, G. Mexi, S. Pokutta and I. Polik published the available coordinate construction for 17 octahedra in a cube in Packing. Haowei Lin's smaller catalogue construction remains the primary result for that case.
  • Contributors whose submissions become records are credited in the record file and on this site.

How to cite

@misc{polyhedrainpolyhedra,
  author       = {Zarzuelo Urdiales, Alejandro},
  title        = {Polyhedra in Polyhedra: certified packings of n copies of a polyhedron in the smallest container},
  year         = {2026},
  howpublished = {\url{https://polyhedrainpolyhedra.wiki}},
  note         = {Records, certificates and code: \url{https://github.com/alejandrozu/platonic-packing}}
}

To cite one packing, give its original author's name, the linked source, its record id (for example ktoinkto_n20) and the date of the record file. The site's maintainer is not the discoverer of every construction.
