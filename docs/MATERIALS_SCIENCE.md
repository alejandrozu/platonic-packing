# Polyhedral packing and materials science: source audit

Research checked 16 October 2026. Public guide: <https://polyhedrainpolyhedra.wiki/materials/>. Its source, full bibliography and numbered citations are in [`web/pages/materials.html`](../web/pages/materials.html).

This is a narrative literature synthesis, not a systematic review or a claim of new experimental materials results. Selection emphasizes primary experiments, simulations and geometric theory that explain a mechanism or test a useful limitation. Publisher articles, openly available author manuscripts and DOI registration metadata were checked; author initials, publication years and physical scope were separately reviewed. Some publisher pages restrict full-text access; the corresponding accessible primary manuscripts were used where available. Tokuda–Furukawa is used only for findings stated in the publisher abstract.

## What the evidence supports

The strongest direct bridge is to assemblies of faceted nanoparticles and colloids, especially under confinement. The page follows the chain **particle/container geometry → collective structure → physical response**, with interactions and processing explicitly included in the final step. Density is one observable among orientation correlations, contact fabric, void topology and defects.

The numbered entries below match the public page. Each DOI links the primary publication; the final column records why the source is used and where the analogy stops.

## Applying the literature to this catalogue

- **Directly provided:** stated finite containers, native positions/rotations, construction lineage and geometry certificates where present. The selected best-found values and alternatives are not a complete phase diagram or a proof of optimality.
- **Proposed analyses:** symmetry-aware orientation correlations, spatial domains, wall-versus-interior structure, layer changes, near-neighbour fabric and probe-dependent pore accessibility. These are not represented as implemented measurements on the new page.
- **Physical follow-up:** interactions, finite-temperature sampling/free energies, assembly dynamics, polydispersity, deformation or loading, and optical/transport calculations as appropriate. A positive certificate gap is not a mechanically load-bearing contact.
- **Grain terminology:** a powder grain is a particle; a crystalline grain is a connected ordered domain. Atomic-lattice and superlattice orientations are separate observables. Classical grain growth concerns a changing tessellation rather than rigid-body packing.
- **Particle-count patterns:** a displayed jump or plateau suggests a comparison to investigate. It establishes neither a phase transition nor the cause of the unknown optimal-size sequence.
- **Dimensions and mixtures:** ordinary material space is 3D. The higher-dimensional mathematical catalogue and future mixed-particle models require explicit additional interpretations.

## Editorial and verification notes

Claims use original paraphrases and link to primary publications. No external figures have been copied. Experiments, simulations, calculated properties and proposed catalogue uses are labelled separately. The older literature page now links the full Teich et al. PNAS article instead of only a conference abstract. Bibliographic checks preserve the 2026 journal year and 2025 first-online date for Huang et al.

The [Lean materials supplement](MATERIALS_FORMALIZATION.md) documents the additional formal connection. It proves geometric sufficient conditions within a stated model, not the experimental findings or thermodynamic theories of these cited papers. Neither the full position-and-orientation partition function nor atomistic alloy/grain-boundary energies are inferred from the new results. The linked source and build evidence identify the exact formal scope.
