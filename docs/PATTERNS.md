# Patterns in the catalogue

_6 November 2026._ The records are not independent optima. They come in plateaus, lattice windows, compositions
and inherited blocks, and many reach their scale by short algebraic contact systems. This page summarizes the
classification. The full account is the paper in [`patterns-paper/`](patterns-paper/) (`patterns.tex`, compiled
`patterns.pdf`). Every number below is generated by [`scripts/patterns/`](../scripts/patterns/) into
[`patterns/stats.json`](patterns/stats.json).

PingYou Ltd introduced much of this view. Their notes on sixteen exact constructions (5 Nov 2026) cover corner
completion, golden shells and layers, recursive composition, exact tilings, Welsh windows, Cartesian and FCC shells,
and mixed, quadratic-field and column layers. Their "arrows" (6 Nov 2026) recover smaller square-antiprism packings
from a larger one.

## The eleven classes

| Class | Mechanism | Formal statement (Lean, `lean-optimality/`) | In the catalogue |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 Deletion, plateaus | any m ≤ n pieces of an n-packing | `Packing.restrict` | 861 plateaus holding 3,397 records; 1,438 records built by deletion |
| P2 Arrows | subset, then F (orientations fixed: an exact LP), then P (all free); insertions and kicks | `Packing.restrict`, `Packing.of_separators` | PingYou's new square-antiprism bounds for n = 3, 5, 6, 7, 8 reproduced from catalogue records |
| P3 Insertion, completion | an (n−1)-packing plus a piece in a hole | constructive | 1,976 records built by insertion |
| P4 Composition | n copies of Q in s C and m of P in t Q give nm of P in (st) C | `Packing.compose`, `Packing.selfCompose`, `Packing.pow` | 15 exact self-composites s(nm) = s(n)s(m); 63 nested records |
| P5 Lattice windows | bodies in Voronoi cells of distinct points | `voronoi_disjoint`, `voronoi_packing`, `lattice_window` | 889 3D records (407 at a rational scale) |
| P6 Layers, orientation classes | few layers, few orientations | descriptive | 1,241 single-orientation 3D records |
| P7 Symmetry | invariance under container symmetries | descriptive | 776 3D records with nontrivial symmetry |
| P8 Algebraic scales | contact equations close the scale | exact certificates | 1,624 closed forms (895 rational, 266 in Q(√2), 185 in Q(√3), 148 in Q(√5)) |
| P9 Extremal | a lower bound meets the construction | `optimal_plateau_of_common_point`, `optimal_plateau_of_one_piece`; `docs/OPTIMALITY.md` | 85 records proven optimal in Lean (centre, facet-width, two-ball, width and box lemmas, volume tilings) |
| P10 Contacts | contact graph; rattlers (pieces touching nothing) | measured | 649 of 2,494 3D records with n ≤ 30 have a rattler |
| P11 Transfer | one piece of K in t K′, or of C′ in λ C | `Packing.transfer_piece`, `Packing.transfer_container` | 19 Platonic records are exactly a transfer; no transfer bound beats a record |

## Exact instances proved in Lean

These are at the exact scales; the certified records carry 10⁻⁷ clearance.

* **FCC windows of rhombic dodecahedra** (`Windows.lean`): every window of the FCC lattice (`rdo_window`).
  Instances: 19 pieces at scale 3 (the shell 1 + 12 + 6), 44 at 4 (offset (½, ½, ½), layers 2 + 8 + 12 + 12 + 8 + 2),
  93 at 5. With deletion (`lattice_windows`), every smaller count follows at the same scale.
* **Cartesian shell of truncated cubes** (`Windows.lean`): the 4 × 4 × 4 grid minus its corners, 56 pieces at
  scale 4 (`tcu56`), hence every n ≤ 56.
* **Triakis recursion** (`Triakis.lean`): 4 triakis tetrahedra at exactly 5/3, hence 4ᵏ at (5/3)ᵏ for every k
  (`triakis_recursion`), including 16 at 25/9 and 64 at 125/27.

## Relations between the classes

Figure: [`patterns-paper/fig_relations.pdf`](patterns-paper/fig_relations.pdf); data:
[`patterns/relations.json`](patterns/relations.json) (`scripts/patterns/relations.py`).

**Formal implications** (Lean, any bodies and containers):

* a lattice window of N pieces gives every n ≤ N at the same scale (`lattice_windows`): windows end plateaus;
* composition with one piece is transfer (`Packing.transfer_piece`, `Packing.transfer_container`);
* deletion and separator certificates make the arrows' F step sound (`Packing.restrict`, `Packing.of_separators`);
* an extremal lower bound plus deletion proves a whole plateau optimal (`optimal_plateau_of_common_point`,
  `optimal_plateau_of_one_piece`, `Relations.lean`).

**Empirical associations.** The lift P(B | A)/P(B) is measured over the 3D records.

* **Rigid cluster.** Windows, single orientation, symmetry, composites and rational scales attract one another
  (lifts 3.6 to 7.2). 77% of windows have one orientation class.
* **Plateaus.** 92% of windows, 94% of frozen subsets and 82% of records with a closed form lie on plateaus.
* **Avoidance.** Rattlers avoid the rigid cluster: in windows the lift is 0.03, and no symmetric record has a
  rattler. Records built by insertion are windows a fifth as often as average.
* **Optimality.** Proven optimality goes with closed forms (lift 5.4).

## Contact graphs and transfer

* `scripts/patterns/contacts.py` builds the contact graph of every 3D record with n ≤ 30
  ([`patterns/contacts3d.json`](patterns/contacts3d.json)). A pair is in contact when its best separating plane
  has margin below 10⁻⁵; a piece touches a wall when a container facet is that close.
* `scripts/patterns/transfer.py` gives the one-piece scales between Platonic solids, for example
  s₁(tet in cube) = 1/√2, s₁(cube in octahedron) = 1 + 1/√2 and s₁(cube in dodecahedron) = 1/φ. It also gives
  every transfer bound between the 25 Platonic families ([`patterns/transfer.json`](patterns/transfer.json)).

## PingYou's arrows

The two steps are defined in their notes; `scripts/patterns/fp.py` implements them.

* **F:** hold every orientation fixed and minimize the scale over the centres. With one separating axis per pair,
  each wall and pair constraint is linear in the centres and the scale, so F is an exact LP whose solutions are
  packings (`Packing.of_separators`).
* **P:** free tightening (the certified sequential LP).

Arrows combine these with deletion (keep a subset), insertion (a body at an affine guess or at the hub) and kicks
(spin a body about its own axis, or rotate a group, by 6°–16°).

`scripts/patterns/arrows_search.py` runs subset arrows. `scripts/patterns/arrows_kick.py` runs insertion and kicks.
Starting only from catalogue records, they reach PingYou's values:

| n | catalogue record | arrow | scale reached | PingYou Ltd |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | 1.973017314 | 3 pieces of n = 15, F, P | 1.9726490831 | 1.972649083082090 |
| 5 | 2.209265853 | 5 pieces of n = 9, F, P | 2.1785815015 | 2.178581501510501 |
| 6 | 2.292893610 | 6 pieces of n = 9, F, P; a 16° spin, F, P | 2.2459217996 | 2.245921799598007 |
| 7 | 2.373563494 | insert into n = 6; four spins | 2.3629758095 | 2.362975810 |
| 8 | 2.402020630 | insert into n = 7; four spins | 2.3849425743 | 2.384942574336931 |

PingYou Ltd has since submitted their constructions for N = 2–20 with exact certificates (PR #107). They are
now the records (N = 15–17 at exactly 3). The same arrows on other antiprism families improved 14 records,
for example hexagonal antiprisms at n = 6 from 2.2466 to 2.1518, and octagonal antiprisms at n = 9 from 2.7464
to 2.6249 and at n = 10 from 2.8515 to 2.7118.

## Rotations, symmetry and subsets in 4D–6D

_7 November 2026._ The 3D measures (orientation classes, relative rotations, configuration symmetry, lattice
windows, subset inheritance) now cover all 1,890 records of the 54 families of regular polytopes in dimensions
4–6 (`scripts/patterns/features_nd.py`, `stats_nd.py`). The symmetry groups are computed from the vertex sets and
checked against their orders: 120 (5-cell), 384 (tesseract, 16-cell), 1152 (24-cell), 14,400 (600-cell,
120-cell), 720, 3,840 and 46,080 (simplex, cube and cross-polytope in 5D and 6D).

| | 3D | 4D | 5D | 6D |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| records | 8,514 | 1,404 | 279 | 207 |
| one orientation class | 14.6% | 17.5% | 15.4% | 15.9% |
| nontrivial configuration symmetry | 9.1% | 20.0% | 22.6% | 24.6% |
| on a plateau | | 21.1% | 17.6% | 22.7% |
| consecutive records frozen (record n inside record n + 1) | 17.9% | 16.4% | 16.7% | 19.7% |
| relative rotations at a rational angle | | 12.6% | 1.1% | 1.4% |
| lattice windows | 889 | 264 | 41 | 12 |

* **Symmetry depends on the container.** Records in simplex containers are almost never symmetric (5-cell 6.8%,
  5-simplex 5.4%, 6-simplex 8.7%), and few have one orientation class (3.8%, 0% and 1.4%). Records in cube containers are the most
  symmetric (tesseract 32.9%, 5-cube 36.6%, 6-cube 36.2%). Several records carry the container's whole group:
  25 24-cells in a 24-cell (order 1152), 10 cross-polytopes in a 5-cross-polytope (3,840) and 12 in a
  6-cross-polytope (46,080): one piece towards each vertex of the container, and one more at the centre of
  the 24-cell.
* **Relative rotations in 4D are isoclinic or simple.** Among pairs of orientation classes, the commonest rational
  rotations are the isoclinic (60°, 60°) (96 pairs), the simple 45° (70), the simple 72° (48), the isoclinic
  (45°, 45°) (35) and the simple 90° (29). The 60° isoclinic rotations are the triality of the 24-cell:
  24 16-cells in a 24-cell (scale √2, symmetry 1152) and 24 16-cells in a 600-cell come in three classes of
  eight, pairwise turned by (60°, 60°): the isoclinic rotations (left multiplication by a unit quaternion of
  order 3 or 6) that permute the three 16-cells inscribed in a 24-cell. The 72° rotations
  come from the 600-cell and 120-cell (H4). In 5D and 6D almost no relative rotation is rational.
* **The rigid cluster is the same as in 3D.** Over the 4D–6D records the lifts are 3.8 (symmetric given one
  orientation class), 2.8 (lattice given symmetric), 3.7 (plateau given lattice), 3.2 (plateau given symmetric)
  and 3.4 (closed form given symmetric).
* **Subsets.** About one consecutive pair in six is frozen in every dimension, as in 3D. The families with the
  most frozen pairs are the cubes (5-cubes 30, tesseracts 29, 6-cubes 22) and the 24-cell (27), where the
  records are grids or shells.

## Data and scripts

* `patterns/features3d.json`: per 3D record, the orientation classes, configuration symmetry (exact and loose),
  layer inventory and lattice test (`scripts/patterns/features.py`).
* `patterns/inherit3d.json`: subset inheritance f(k, N), the share of record k found rigidly inside record N
  (`scripts/patterns/inherit.py`).
* `patterns/stats.json`: everything above, aggregated (`scripts/patterns/stats.py`).
* `patterns/features_nd.json`, `patterns/inherit_nd.json`, `patterns/stats_nd.json`: the same measures for the
  4D–6D records, with the relative rotation angles of each pair of orientation classes
  (`scripts/patterns/features_nd.py`, `stats_nd.py`).
* `patterns-paper/`: the manuscript, its figures (`scripts/patterns/figures.py`) and the generated macros and
  tables (`scripts/patterns/paper_macros.py`).
