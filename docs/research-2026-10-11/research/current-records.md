# Current finite-container record comparison

Checked 11 October 2026. All values use container edge divided by unit particle edge. Smaller is better. These are the best retrieved comparable bounds, with exact constructions, certified stored coordinates, and truncated external listings distinguished.

The companion JSON covers all 99 cells of the two proposed tables. For the seven families beyond cubes in cubes and octahedra in cubes, no competing finite-container bounds were located in the targeted search; this supports reporting the user's archived bounds with dated scope, rather than unqualified global-record or optimality claims.

## Source/precision decisions

- Cubes 2–8: elementary grid edge 2. The local exact verifier uses small positive gaps, so its stored edge is slightly larger. The volume lower bound proves optimality at 8; this audit does not claim corresponding proofs for 2–7.
- Cubes 9–10: Friedman's 1998 construction at 2+1/√2; the user supplies a certified reproduction.
- Cubes 11: the user improves Lin's published 2.8829529104956513 to certified 2.8817115130006 (touching estimate 2.881711124829), starting from Lin's configuration. Ryan's new announcement reports a tie.
- Cubes 12: Nakajima's geometric construction; his released strictly separated side is 2.9315185094797 with 1e-6 clearance. The user's smaller stored side primarily reflects 1e-7 clearance. Credit the origin to Nakajima and provide both sources.
- Octahedra 2–10: the catalogue credits Walsh. The local certified witnesses match the displayed numbers, supplying inspectable coordinates while retaining Walsh's attribution.
- Octahedra 11–12: Walsh's 2.19540+ and Lin's 2.25949+ beat the user's values. These are truncated listings, not exact floating-point scales. Their source images are linked. No public poses or separate originating papers were found, so their rotating exact configurations cannot be fabricated.

## Wikipedia and historical scope

Relevant existing coverage is Packing problems, Sphere packing in a cube, Square packing, and articles about cube tiling/puzzles. A cube-specific expansion within the new article is the appropriate local deliverable. The suggested title Polyhedra in polyhedra packing is descriptive and correctly identifies the broad finite topic; scholarly use of the exact title has not been established.

See cube-history-and-methods.md for approximately 1,000 words of link-rich history and methodology. Friedman's 1998 listings support a historical interval for specific numerical records; generic polyhedral packing methods in 2009 and 2018 rule out a broad claim of field-wide stagnation.
