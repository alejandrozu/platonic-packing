## Scope, title, and existing coverage

The selected title is **Polyhedra in polyhedra packing**. It describes both the particles and their finite containers without claiming a new technical term has already been adopted throughout the literature. The current scope differs from infinite polyhedral packing, single-polyhedron inscription, sphere packing in a cube, and cube-passage problems.

## Sources and the new research material

The historical literature and general definitions have independently authored scholarly sources. Friedman's catalogues are primary attributed sources for their printed constructions. Nakajima's repository and paper are primary descriptions of his cube procedure. Zarzuelo Urdiales's two companion papers, immutable records, and analysis describe the generalized method and the 351 configurations.

The new archive analysis is explicitly attributed to its author in the article. Its conjectures are distinguished from measured patterns and exact feasibility proofs. This makes the repository article useful and inspectable, but does not settle whether Wikipedia will accept all of that material as encyclopedic content. A self-authored manuscript or GitHub deposit is still a primary source; publication or a DOI alone does not create independent secondary coverage.

[Wikipedia's no-original-research policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:No_original_research) requires published reliable sources and bars new interpretations that those sources do not state. Straightforward statements from primary sources can sometimes be used cautiously. Evaluative claims that the recent methods are the first general packing algorithms, ended stagnation across the field, or constitute a widely recognized breakthrough require appropriate independent sourcing. Earlier arbitrary-polytope and continuous-rotation methods make a blanket novelty claim inaccurate in any event.

[The notability guideline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Notability) governs whether the finite-container topic warrants a separate article, generally expecting significant independent coverage. Primary descriptions may support individual constructions without establishing standalone notability or the weight that should be assigned to a contributor. The current article is the user's detailed repository article as well as proposed encyclopedia source; final Wikipedia source selection and weighting need independent assessment.

## Record comparisons and construction provenance

The two result tables use a dated best-retrieved comparison and link every entry to its source. They do not equate numerical records with proved optima. Rounded catalogue values, numerical touching limits, and exact certified positive-clearance scales are different evidence types. Preserve all three distinctions when revising a row.

External records are credited to their originating author and paper where located. Where only a catalogue or coordinate artifact is available, cite that source honestly; do not invent a paper or silently attribute the number to the generalized project. An external numerical row without retrieved coordinates cannot be illustrated by a different construction while claiming the image represents that row. The particularly relevant twelve-cube refinement shares Nakajima's touching family; reducing certification clearance is not a new touching-scale improvement.

The dodecahedral 29–33 plateau is an exact equality of stored feasible scales. Its common shell geometry, approximate pose matches, contact angles, and shell classification are floating-point structural observations. Neither establishes that all five true optima coincide. The suggested radical touching scale still needs an exact-contact reconstruction. These distinctions should survive any shortening of the article.
