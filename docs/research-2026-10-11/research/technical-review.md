## Mathematical conclusions

1. The soft-shape nesting proof is valid for any compact convex body containing the centered radius-rho ball. It concerns the shapes and globally defined feasible problems. It does not guarantee a feasible or monotone local optimizer trajectory during sharpening.
2. The signed rounded-distance identity is valid when penetration means the shortest separating translation. The signed value is minus the minimum support value of the Minkowski difference over unit directions. Ball dilation adds 2r to that support value. Unsigned distance requires the nonnegative maximum when the cores are disjoint.
3. The kernel's minimum of the two directional projected depths handles asymmetric pieces and interval containment correctly. Interval-intersection length is not the required separating translation. The SAT axis collection includes every Minkowski-difference facet direction; its largest positive gap is generally a Euclidean-distance lower bound, not the distance itself.
4. The exact certificate uses rational quaternion rotations and exact field signs. A proposed separator has a rational normal and generally algebraic midpoint offset. Floating choices are subsequently checked against every exact vertex. The certificate proves the stored dyadic `s_full`, not `s_tight`, a fitted radical, or global optimality.
5. Paper 2's orientation reduction is correct. For independently chosen symmetry representatives, cyclicity reduces trace(g_i^T R_i^T R_j g_j) to trace(R_i^T R_j g_j g_i^T); group closure therefore reduces the two-group minimum to one group. The implementation maximizes trace before applying arccos, exactly the required minimization. It uses proper shape symmetries on the correct side of the local-to-world orientation matrix.
6. Proper symmetry groups have the expected orders 12, 24, 24, 60, and 60. Every product of group elements was checked against the generated set: maximum closure discrepancy was 5.72e-16 in Frobenius norm. Across 20 random trials per shape, common global rotation and independent right multiplication by shape symmetries changed pair disorientation by at most 2.21e-13 degrees.
7. The Paper 2 segment-distance equations have the correct signs, and the endpoint and interior cases cover disjoint convex-polytope feature minima. Circumsphere and SAT rejection are valid contact-pruning bounds. Both operands share one canonical piece type within each record, as required by the function interface.
8. The volume bound, deletion envelope, density normalization, cubic volume increments, and density-ratio identities are correct. Both manuscripts consistently use A for container and B for piece. The deletion envelope proves an upper bound even if the raw record sequence is nonmonotone.
9. Single-linkage orientation components need not be pairwise uniform. Paper 2 records component diameter and separately counts fully pairwise aligned records. Its grain proxy requires near contact and shape-orientation similarity and is explicitly distinct from physical crystal grains or positional lattice order.
10. The constructive tetrahedral and octahedral counts and containment/separation arguments are valid. They prove feasible touching constructions, not optimality at all corresponding counts.
