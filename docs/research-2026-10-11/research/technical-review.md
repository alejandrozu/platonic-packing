## Scope and fresh validation

The review inspected the search kernel, dynamics, numerical tightener, claim preparation, exact checker, constructive seeds, autonomous engine, and the Paper 2 analysis. The full historical search was not repeated. The [fresh certificate replay](verification.json) records all 351 frozen configurations passing exact containment/separation, the floating checker, and declared-population versus actual-pose-count validation, with zero failures in 351.38 elapsed seconds. The original geometry test also passed all 1,500 gradient cases, with 731 separated and 769 penetrating pairs and maximum smooth finite-difference discrepancy approximately 1.43e-9.

A separate review compared the Paper 2 exhaustive feature-distance implementation with the repository's slab-pruned JavaScript kernel. A fixed random seed of 20261011 generated 500 same-piece pairs for each of the five solids. Of the 2,500 random requests, 2,071 were separated and eligible for a Euclidean-distance comparison. The review additionally tested 1,011 near pairs from every n=40 record, covering all nine piece/container families. All 3,082 compared distances agreed to at most 1.9984e-15 piece-edge units. There were zero contact-threshold classification differences at 1e-5, 1e-4, or 1e-3. All actual near-record requests were separated. The [machine-readable crosscheck](geometry-crosscheck.json) preserves counts and errors.

This is numerical agreement between different implementations of the same feature geometry, not an exact interval bound for every structural descriptor. The record certificate provides the independent exact feasibility proof; distance and orientation measurements remain floating-point analyses.

## Mathematical conclusions

1. The soft-shape nesting proof is valid for any compact convex body containing the centered radius-rho ball. It concerns the shapes and globally defined feasible problems. It does not guarantee a feasible or monotone local optimizer trajectory during sharpening.
2. The signed rounded-distance identity is valid when penetration means the shortest separating translation. The signed value is minus the minimum support value of the Minkowski difference over unit directions. Ball dilation adds 2r to that support value. Unsigned distance requires the nonnegative maximum when the cores are disjoint.
3. The kernel's minimum of the two directional projected depths handles asymmetric pieces and interval containment correctly. Interval-intersection length is not the required separating translation. The SAT axis collection includes every Minkowski-difference facet direction; its largest positive gap is generally a Euclidean-distance lower bound, not the distance itself.
4. The exact certificate uses rational quaternion rotations and exact field signs. A proposed separator has a rational normal and generally algebraic midpoint offset. Floating choices are subsequently checked against every exact vertex. The certificate proves the stored dyadic `s_full`, not `s_tight`, a fitted radical, or global optimality.
5. Paper 2's orientation reduction is correct. For independently chosen symmetry representatives, cyclicity reduces trace(g_i^T R_i^T R_j g_j) to trace(R_i^T R_j g_j g_i^T); group closure therefore reduces the two-group minimum to one group. The implementation maximizes trace before applying arccos, exactly the required minimization. It uses proper shape symmetries on the correct side of the local-to-world orientation matrix.
6. Proper symmetry groups have the expected orders 12, 24, 24, 60, and 60. Every product of group elements was checked against the generated set: maximum closure discrepancy was 5.72e-16 in Frobenius norm. Across 20 random trials per shape, common global rotation and independent right multiplication by shape symmetries changed pair disorientation by at most 2.21e-13 degrees.
7. The Paper 2 segment-distance equations have the correct signs, and the endpoint and interior cases cover disjoint convex-polytope feature minima. Circumsphere and SAT rejection are valid contact-pruning bounds. Both operands share one canonical piece type within each record, as required by the function interface.
8. The volume bound, deletion envelope, density normalization, cubic volume increments, and density-ratio identities are correct. Both manuscripts consistently use A for container and B for piece. The deletion envelope proves an upper bound even if the raw record sequence is nonmonotone.
9. Single-linkage orientation components need not be pairwise uniform. Paper 2 records component diameter and separately counts fully pairwise aligned records. Its grain proxy requires near contact and shape-orientation similarity and is explicitly distinct from physical crystal grains or positional lattice order.
10. The constructive tetrahedral and octahedral counts and containment/separation arguments are valid. They prove feasible touching constructions, not optimality at all corresponding counts.

## Corrections incorporated in the methods manuscript

The methods paper distinguishes exact geometric formulas from their floating evaluation and from the exact final checker. It reports the actual factor-two container wall update rather than claiming literal descent of the displayed energy. It corrects rounding-radius units, the top-three randomized deletion rule, stale first-night sizes, predominantly SLP-based tightening, and the limited scope of the target pair-clearance shortcut. It distinguishes logged worker elapsed hours from measured CPU utilization. It states that coordinate fields do not constrain the algebraic form of unknown optimal values. It treats the one-hour randomized comparison as an adaptive shared-archive experiment with overlapping rate intervals, rather than a universal significance result.

## Resolved review item

The initial plateau detector compared each numerical value with the first value in the prospective run, whereas the methods text specified a full max-minus-min criterion. This has been corrected. Direct review of `plateau_runs` confirms that it maintains a running minimum and maximum and splits when the proposed whole interval exceeds tolerance. Its self-test exercises the adversarial nonmonotone sequence `[0, 0.75, -0.75]` with tolerance 1, retaining the first two values together and rejecting the third from that interval. The analysis was regenerated and its 58 reported plateau intervals remained unchanged. No scientific correctness issue identified by this review remains unresolved.

## Practical limits preserved in both papers

The manuscripts do not claim complete coverage of all 25 Platonic pairs, joint two-species packings, global optimality, a verified exact-contact form for conjectural radicals, thermodynamic crystallinity, a force-bearing contact network, or a world-record comparison continuously updated after the data freeze. The second paper's correlations are descriptive across one selected winner per count and retain count dependence, archive ancestry, threshold sensitivity, and lack of independent sampling as limitations. These are material boundaries of the evidence, rather than reasons to discard the certified construction dataset.
