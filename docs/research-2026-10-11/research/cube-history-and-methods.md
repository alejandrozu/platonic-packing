# Cubes in cubes: history, records, and the transition to a general atlas

Evidence checked on 11 October 2026. This text concerns a finite population of congruent, rigid particles in a hard-walled polyhedral container. “Best retrieved” below identifies a dated comparison, not a proof that every publication or newly announced configuration has been found.

## The finite cube problem and its conventions

For \(n\) unit-edge cubes, let \(s_3(n)\) be the infimum of the edge of a containing cube over translations and independent rotations with pairwise disjoint interiors. A concrete arrangement establishes an upper bound on \(s_3(n)\). Proving optimality additionally requires a matching lower bound that excludes every smaller arrangement. Merely converging to the same arrangement repeatedly does not supply that lower bound. The objective differs from packing differently sized boxes into several bins, from tiling unbounded space, and from passing one cube through a hole in another cube.

An elementary construction places cubes in an axis-aligned \(k\times k\times k\) grid. Removing cubes gives a feasible container of edge \(k\) for every \(n\leq k^3\). The volume inequality gives \(s_3(n)\geq n^{1/3}\). At \(n=k^3\), these bounds coincide and prove \(s_3(k^3)=k\); for other populations the volume bound generally leaves a gap. The axis-aligned grid itself has no need for independent particle rotations, while improved intermediate-count constructions exploit space unavailable to that restricted family. These observations are elementary deductions, rather than additional record claims.

[Erich Friedman's Cubes in Cubes catalogue](https://erich-friedman.github.io/packing/cubincub/) gives a particularly concrete history of the finite problem. It credits constructions for 9–10 cubes at \(2+1/\sqrt2\), 13 cubes at the displayed \(2.956+\), 14 at \(2+7\sqrt2/10\), and 28–33 at \(3+1/\sqrt2\) to Friedman in 1998. The current page lists later 11- and 12-cube improvements credited to Haowei Lin in July 2026. These dates document a long interval between particular recorded constructions. They do not establish that the entire field of packing experienced three decades of stagnation.

## Earlier computational work and the 2026 record chain

General-purpose polyhedral packing methods predate the recent atlas. [Egeblad, Nielsen, and Brazil (2009)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.comgeo.2008.06.003) optimized translational placements of arbitrary polytopes. [Romanova, Bennell, Stoyan, and Pankratov (2018)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejor.2018.01.025) modeled concave polyhedra with continuous rotations in a minimum-volume cuboid. [Callisaya and Kari (2025, published online in 2026)](https://doi.org/10.5540/tcam.2025.026.e01823) formulated congruent-polytope fitting in bounded convex domains using nonlinear optimization and separating hyperplanes. Their objectives, particle classes, and experimental containers differ. Their existence means that the recent contribution should be described as a specific generalization, dataset, and analysis, rather than the invention of generic polyhedron fitting.

Lin's public [Hyra packing artifacts](https://github.com/Tencent-Hunyuan/Hyra-results/tree/09b2d2e3c362c67a78f544c836bd8e4b54a05527/AI4Science/packing_records) give explicit full-precision poses for 11 cubes at [2.8829529104956513](https://github.com/Tencent-Hunyuan/Hyra-results/blob/09b2d2e3c362c67a78f544c836bd8e4b54a05527/AI4Science/packing_records/records/cubincub_n11.json) and 12 cubes at [2.9327717687048653](https://github.com/Tencent-Hunyuan/Hyra-results/blob/09b2d2e3c362c67a78f544c836bd8e4b54a05527/AI4Science/packing_records/records/cubincub_n12.json). Public coordinate files improve on a picture-only record: another researcher can reconstruct the exact orientation convention, test feasibility, and use the arrangement as a search seed. A link to an artifact is not a substitute for a journal paper; no separate Lin paper establishing these two numerical records was located in this check.

## Nakajima's continuation method

[Yohei Nakajima's soft-to-rigid-packing project](https://github.com/yoheinakajima/soft-to-rigid-packing/tree/efe7a920142350a4f0678633490c3e3dbed71fa0) introduced the immediate computational precursor used here. It compresses balls, hardens them through rounded cubes while the container remains under inward pressure, then settles and tightens rigid cubes. A rounded unit cube of softness \(r\in[0,1/2]\) is \((1-2r)[-1/2,1/2]^3\oplus B(0,r)\). The endpoints are an inscribed ball and a unit cube. The intermediate bodies remain inside the final cube, so continuing from soft particles changes the obstacles and the routes through configuration space without changing the final rigid target.

The project couples analytic rigid-motion gradients and compression dynamics with numerical legalization, constrained SLSQP tightening, and independent exact verification. Nakajima's [paper](https://github.com/yoheinakajima/soft-to-rigid-packing/blob/efe7a920142350a4f0678633490c3e3dbed71fa0/paper/main.pdf), [released coordinates](https://github.com/yoheinakajima/soft-to-rigid-packing/blob/efe7a920142350a4f0678633490c3e3dbed71fa0/claims/cubincub_n12/cubincub_n12.json), and [version DOI](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23248095) document 12 unit cubes in side 2.9315185094797 with pair and wall clearance of at least \(10^{-6}\). The reported touching estimate is 2.931514577965. Those are different quantities: one is a strictly separated stored configuration and the other a numerical limiting estimate.

## Zarzuelo's generalization and structural analysis

[Alejandro Zarzuelo Urdiales's platonic-packing repository](https://github.com/alejandrozu/platonic-packing) extends this architecture to five same-solid pairs and the four ordered cube–octahedron and dodecahedron–icosahedron dual pairs. The [frozen mathematical description](https://github.com/alejandrozu/platonic-packing/blob/e15b496ae7306287e0e70bb5b14be79f5dfdde84/docs/MATH.md) replaces the rounded cube by \(S_r=(1-r/\rho)P\oplus B(0,r)\), where \(P\) is the centered particle and \(\rho\) its inradius. A half-space container description, general polyhedral distance computations, local tightening, archive perturbations, and removal-and-reinsertion searches supply a reusable implementation across those nine families. Generality of the shape formula and coverage of the published experiment are distinct: this dataset does not contain all 25 ordered Platonic pairs.

The [351 configurations for \(n=2,\ldots,40\)](https://github.com/alejandrozu/platonic-packing/blob/e15b496ae7306287e0e70bb5b14be79f5dfdde84/records/SUMMARY.csv) have exact feasibility certificates over \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt2,\sqrt5)\). The [checker](https://github.com/alejandrozu/platonic-packing/blob/e15b496ae7306287e0e70bb5b14be79f5dfdde84/certify_exact.py) treats stored numerical values as exact rationals, constructs exactly orthogonal quaternion rotations, verifies every particle vertex against every container face, and checks a strict separating plane for every particle pair. It certifies the represented arrangement, not the unknown global optimum.

The scientific extension also includes structural questions: count-dependent jumps and plateaus; orientation comparisons reduced by the particle's rotational symmetries; near-contact graphs; and connected orientation domains, operationally called grains. This turns an atlas of coordinates into a testable account of how construction families behave. In particular, a capacity plateau can coexist with extensive rearrangement or with removal of non-load-bearing particles. These alternatives are hypotheses to investigate with geometry and repeated search; they are not established crystallization dynamics. The current 11-cube [stored configuration](https://github.com/alejandrozu/platonic-packing/blob/e15b496ae7306287e0e70bb5b14be79f5dfdde84/records/cubincub/cubincub_n11.json), found after using Lin's arrangement as a seed, has certified side 2.8817115130006. The 12-cube result reproduces Nakajima's touching family with a smaller clearance convention, which should be credited as reproduction rather than a new geometric improvement.
