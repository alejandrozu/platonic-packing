Literature

The papers and sources behind this site: the project's own write-ups, the work it starts from and compares against, and the research literature on packing as an optimization problem, on dense packings of polyhedra and on proving packings correct. Each entry says in one line how it relates to the method used here.

In short: the project's local optimizer solves the same model as the quasi-phi-function literature (a separating plane between every pair of pieces), with linear-programming steps in place of a full nonlinear solve, plus exact certification, soft-to-rigid starting points and regular polytopes in dimensions 4–6. The closest work on the same problems is Berthold, Kamp, Mexi, Pokutta and Pólik (2026), which treats all 25 Platonic pairs for n ≤ 30 with global optimization solvers. No published container values were found for the 4–6-dimensional problems.

Where the project starts, and what it compares against

  • Y. Nakajima (2026). soft-to-rigid-packing: the soft-to-rigid method for packing unit cubes in a cube (12 cubes in a cube of side 2.9315), which this project generalizes.
  • E. Friedman. Erich's Packing Center: catalogue values for cubes in a cube (n = 2–33) and octahedra in a cube (n = 2–30), with constructions by E. Friedman, R. Walsh, H. Lin and others; the only Platonic problems with a catalogue.
  • T. Berthold, D. Kamp, G. Mexi, S. Pokutta, I. Pólik (2026). Out-of-the-box global optimization for packing problems: new models and improved solutions, arXiv:2605.04850, with a coordinate supplement: all 25 Platonic pairs for n ≤ 30 with the global solvers SCIP and FICO Xpress; non-overlap through Farkas multipliers (the dual form of a separating plane); feasible solutions for 429 of 750 instances, and lower bounds above the volume bound only for n ≤ 2. Sizes there are outer circumradii with unit-circumradius pieces; this site's s = R · ρpiece/ρcontainer, ρ = circumradius ÷ edge.
  • Tencent Hunyuan. Hyra-results: published coordinates for cubes and octahedra in a cube.
  • R. Xu. square_packing: constructions for 34–36 cubes in a cube.
  • E. Friedman (2009). Packing unit squares in squares: a survey and new results, Electronic Journal of Combinatorics, Dynamic Survey DS7: the two-dimensional analogue (n ≤ 100, several proven optima); the pattern s = k + 1/√2 for one more than a perfect square reappears for cubes.
  • Community submissions: credited in each record file and on the records page.

Packing as an optimization problem

Operations research writes non-overlap as explicit constraints and solves the result with nonlinear, linear or integer programming. The best single entry point is the review by Leao et al. (2020) below.

Phi-functions and quasi-phi-functions

A phi-function of two placed objects is positive when they are apart, zero when they touch and negative when they overlap; a quasi-phi-function adds auxiliary variables (for two convex polytopes: a separating plane) so the constraints stay smooth. This is exactly the per-pair constraint of this project's tightening step.

  • Y. G. Stoyan, M. V. Novozhilova, A. V. Kartashov (1996). Mathematical model and method of searching for a local extremum for the non-convex oriented polygons allocation problem. European Journal of Operational Research 92(1), 193–210. Local optimization as an active set over the binding pairwise inequalities: the ancestor of constraint generation in the tightening.
  • Y. G. Stoyan, N. I. Gil, G. Scheithauer, A. Pankratov, I. Magdalina (2005). Packing of convex polytopes into a parallelepiped. Optimization, 215–235. The first 3D polytope-in-box optimization model of this line of work.
  • J. Bennell, G. Scheithauer, Y. Stoyan, T. Romanova (2010). Tools of mathematical modeling of arbitrary object packing problems. Annals of Operations Research 179(1), 343–368. Tutorial: phi-functions, Minkowski sums, no-fit polygons and continuous rotation.
  • A. V. Pankratov, T. E. Romanova, A. M. Chugay (2015). Optimal packing of convex polytopes using quasi-phi-functions. Journal of Mechanical Engineering 18(2), 55–64. Convex polytopes in a minimal cuboid with continuous rotation, one separating plane per pair, solved with IPOPT: the same constraint system as this project's tightening, which updates the plane normal on its tangent plane and takes linear-programming steps instead.
  • Y. Stoyan, T. Romanova, A. Pankratov, A. Chugay (2015). Optimized object packings using quasi-phi-functions. In Optimized Packings with Applications, Springer, 265–293. Introduces quasi-phi-functions for 2D and 3D objects.
  • Y. Stoyan, A. Pankratov, T. Romanova (2016). Cutting and packing problems for irregular objects with continuous rotations: mathematical modelling and non-linear optimization. Journal of the Operational Research Society 67(5), 786–800.
  • T. Romanova, J. Bennell, Y. Stoyan, A. Pankratov (2018). Packing of concave polyhedra with continuous rotations using nonlinear optimisation. European Journal of Operational Research 268(1), 37–53. The flagship 3D paper: non-convex polyhedra as unions of convex parts, compaction by decomposition into smaller nonlinear programs.
  • Y. E. Stoian, A. M. Chugay, A. V. Pankratov, T. E. Romanova (2018). Two approaches to modeling and solving the packing problem for convex polytopes. Cybernetics and Systems Analysis 54(4), 585–593. Phi- against quasi-phi-function models: the case for the separating-plane form.
  • M. S. Sofronova (2007). Mathematical modeling of the placement of convex n-dimensional polytopes in an n-dimensional parallelepiped. CSc thesis, Kharkiv. The only optimization formulation found for polytopes in dimensions above 3.

Separating planes, nonlinear and global optimization

  • J. Peralta, M. Andretta, J. F. Oliveira (2018). Solving irregular strip packing problems with free rotations using separation lines. Pesquisa Operacional 38(2), 195–214. The 2D twin of the separating-plane model, with freely rotating pieces and lines.
  • L. H. Cherri, A. C. Cherri, E. M. Soler (2018). Mixed integer quadratically-constrained programming model to solve the irregular strip packing problem with continuous rotations. Journal of Global Optimization 72, 89–107. Exact global solution for small instances; its symmetry breaking suits small-n optimality experiments.
  • J. Kallrath (2017). Packing ellipsoids into volume-minimizing rectangular boxes. Journal of Global Optimization 67(1), 151–185. Separating hyperplanes with arbitrary orientations; global solvers stall well before n ≈ 10.
  • E. G. Birgin, R. D. Lobato, J. M. Martínez (2016). Packing ellipsoids by nonlinear optimization. Journal of Global Optimization 65(4), 709–743. Separating-hyperplane models in any dimension, as in the 4–6-dimensional problems here.
  • P. Rocha, R. Rodrigues, A. M. Gomes, F. M. B. Toledo, M. Andretta (2014). Circle covering representation for nesting problems with continuous rotations. 19th IFAC World Congress, 5235–5240. A smooth proxy first, then the hard model and an LP repair: close in spirit to soft-to-rigid.

Linear programming compaction and integer programming

  • Z. Li, V. Milenkovic (1995). Compaction and separation algorithms for non-convex polygons and their applications. European Journal of Operational Research 84(3), 539–561. Linear programming over positions with locally fixed separating constraints: the origin of the tightening step.
  • A. M. Gomes, J. F. Oliveira (2006). Solving irregular strip packing problems by hybridising simulated annealing and linear programming. European Journal of Operational Research 171(3), 811–829. Stochastic outer search with an LP polish: the same architecture as basin hopping plus tightening.
  • T. Imamichi, M. Yagiura, H. Nagamochi (2007). An iterated local search algorithm based on nonlinear programming for the irregular strip packing problem. Technical Report 2007-009, Kyoto University. Overlap minimization and swap moves: the 2D counterpart of the soft phase.
  • M. Fischetti, I. Luzzi (2009). Mixed-integer programming models for nesting problems. Journal of Heuristics 15, 201–226. Integer choice of the active facet of the no-fit region: proven-optimal translations for a fixed set of orientations.
  • L. H. Cherri, L. R. Mundim, M. Andretta, F. M. B. Toledo, J. F. Oliveira, M. A. Carravilla (2016). Robust mixed-integer linear programming models for the irregular strip packing problem. European Journal of Operational Research 253(3), 570–583.
  • J. J. Lastra-Díaz, M. T. Ortuño (2024). Mixed-integer programming models for irregular strip packing based on vertical slices and feasibility cuts. European Journal of Operational Research 313(1), 69–91. Proven optima reach about 20–30 pieces in 2D with translations only.
  • J. Egeblad, B. K. Nielsen, A. Odgaard (2007). Fast neighborhood search for two- and three-dimensional nesting problems. European Journal of Operational Research 183(3), 1249–1266. Exact one-dimensional overlap minimization: a cheap move in any dimension.

Dense packings of polyhedra

Infinite-space packing densities give a sanity check for large n, s ≈ (n · vol B / (φ · vol A))1/3; they are not bounds.

  • S. Torquato, Y. Jiao (2009). Dense packings of the Platonic and Archimedean solids. Nature 460, 876–879; long version Physical Review E 80, 041104. The adaptive shrinking cell: a container that shrinks while the pieces relax. Densities: octahedra 18/19, dodecahedra (5 + √5)/8, icosahedra ≈ 0.836.
  • S. Torquato, Y. Jiao (2010). Robust algorithm to generate a diverse class of dense disordered and ordered sphere packings via linear programming. Physical Review E 82, 061302. Sequential linear programming for spheres in dimensions 2–6: the physics analogue of the tightening step.
  • A. Haji-Akbari, M. Engel, A. S. Keys, X. Zheng, R. G. Petschek, P. Palffy-Muhoray, S. C. Glotzer (2009). Disordered, quasicrystalline and crystalline phases of densely packed tetrahedra. Nature 462, 773–777.
  • Y. Kallus, V. Elser, S. Gravel (2010). Dense periodic packings of tetrahedra with small repeating units. Discrete & Computational Geometry 44(2), 245–252.
  • E. R. Chen, M. Engel, S. C. Glotzer (2010). Dense crystalline dimer packings of regular tetrahedra. Discrete & Computational Geometry 44(2), 253–280. The densest known tetrahedron packing, 4000/4671 ≈ 0.8563.
  • S. Gravel, V. Elser (2008). Divide and concur: a general approach to constraint satisfaction. Physical Review E 78, 036706; and Y. Kallus, V. Elser, S. Gravel (2010), Method for dense packing discovery, Physical Review E 82, 056707. A projection-based global search; a 4-simplex packing of density 128/219, the reference for the 5-cell problems here.
  • E. R. Chen, D. Klotsa, M. Engel, P. F. Damasceno, S. C. Glotzer (2014). Complexity in surfaces of densest packings for families of polyhedra. Physical Review X 4, 011024. Densest packings across families between Platonic, Archimedean and Catalan solids.
  • J. C. Lagarias, C. Zong (2012). Mysteries in packing regular tetrahedra. Notices of the AMS 59(11).
  • E. G. Teich, G. van Anders, D. Klotsa, J. Dshemuchadse, S. C. Glotzer (2016). Clusters of polyhedra in spherical confinement. Up to about 60 Platonic solids in a sphere: a possible new container class with published values.

Proofs, certification and complexity

  • M. Cs. Markót, T. Csendes (2005). A new verified optimization technique for the "packing circles in a unit square" problems. SIAM Journal on Optimization 16(1), 193–219; and M. Cs. Markót (2021), Improved interval methods for solving circle packing problems in the unit square, Journal of Global Optimization 81(3), 773–803. Interval proofs of optimality reach 33 circles in a square; this site proves that each packing is valid, not that it is optimal.
  • M. Abrahamsen, T. Miltzow, N. Seiferth (2020). Framework for ∃ℝ-completeness of two-dimensional packing problems. FOCS 2020, 1014–1021. Optimal packings with rotations may need algebraic numbers of unbounded degree: the reason the certificates use a small positive clearance in a fixed number field.

Open ground

  • Published values exist only for cubes and octahedra in a cube (n ≤ 33 and n ≤ 30) and, from Berthold et al., for the 25 Platonic pairs with n ≤ 30. For n up to 100, for the Archimedean, Catalan and prism families and for the regular polytopes in dimensions 4–6, the values on this site appear to be the first published.
  • No optimality is known for n ≥ 3 beyond trivial grids (for example k3 cubes in a cube of side k).
  • Better packings, missing references and corrections are welcome: contribute or open an issue.
