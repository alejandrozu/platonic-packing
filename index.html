How small can the container be? Platonic solids in Platonic solids

Each row is the smallest container found for n unit-edge copies of a solid (n = 2–40; each solid in itself, cubes in a cube, and the four dual pairs), reported as s = container edge ÷ piece edge. Pieces start as their inscribed spheres and are sharpened into the solid under pressure (Yohei Nakajima's soft-to-rigid method), then improved by basin hopping and tightened. Every packing is proven valid in exact arithmetic over ℚ(√2, √5).

Bars: s(n)/s(n − 1) − 1 in percent (left axis). Line: s(n) − s(n − 1) in piece edges (right axis). Both use the touching limits (no clearance). Shaded columns: no increase, the extra piece fits in the same container.

Inscribed sphere first

Every piece starts as the ball inscribed in it. The intermediate shape is (1 − r/ρ)·P ⊕ B(r): it always fits inside the final solid and only grows as r falls, so the search follows one monotone path from sphere packing to polytope packing.

Basin hopping

From a tightened packing the container is expanded, the pieces softened and shaken until their relative arrangement has changed, then re-sharpened and re-tightened (sequential LP, then SLSQP). Adding a piece to the n − 1 packing or removing one from the n + 1 packing keeps s(n) ≤ s(n + 1) for every n.

Proven, not just plotted

certify_exact.py rebuilds each unit-edge solid with coordinates in ℚ(√2, √5), checks every vertex is strictly inside the container and exhibits a separating plane for every pair, with no rounding. A row is an upper bound with a proof; optimality is not claimed.

Earlier records

Only two of the nine problems have published values, both on Erich Friedman's Packing Center: cubes in a cube (n < 34) and octahedra in a cube (n = 1–30). Their tables carry a previous column; “better” means more than 10⁻⁵ below the listed value, which is often a 5-decimal truncation.

Reading the viewer

Orientation colours pieces that are exact translates of each other alike, so lattice layers and repeated motifs stand out. Slice keeps only the pieces whose centres lie below a level along one container axis. Clicking a piece lists its centre and its nearest neighbours; isolate keeps just that neighbourhood.
